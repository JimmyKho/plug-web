Wiring 101: Malaysian domestic wiring from the supply to the socket

What a wireman needs in their head before opening a backbox. Read this once, then go and break things safely on the bench.

This primer does not make you a wireman. In Malaysia, fixed electrical work must be done or supervised by a wireman registered with Suruhanjaya Tenaga (the Energy Commission), under the Electricity Supply Act 1990 and the Electricity Regulations 1994. Use this to understand your installation and to learn. Never work on a live circuit.

1. How power reaches the house

Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB) delivers single-phase 240 V at 50 Hz to most terrace, flat and apartment units, and three-phase 415 V to larger homes with heavy air-conditioning or EV charging. The service cable lands at the meter, then the main switch and consumer unit (distribution board, often just called the DB).

Inside the consumer unit the supply passes through a residual current device (RCD, still widely called an ELCB) and is then split into final circuits, each protected by a miniature circuit breaker (MCB). Every circuit on the bench starts at one of those MCBs.

Older houses use a TT earthing arrangement with a local earth rod. Newer supplies are commonly TNC-S (PME), where TNB provides the earth. Either way, every final circuit carries its own protective conductor back to the earth bar.

Two rules fall straight out of this table. Only the live is switched or fused. The earth never carries load current.

3. Colour code, old and new

Malaysia adopted the IEC harmonised colours (MS IEC 60364, with cables to MS 2112 carrying the SIRIM mark). Older installations, roughly pre-2008, still use the previous British scheme. You will meet both, sometimes in the same house.

ConductorHarmonised (current)Old scheme
LiveBrownRed
NeutralBlueBlack
EarthGreen / yellowGreen (or bare, to be sleeved)
Switched liveBrown, or blue sleeved brown at both endsRed, or black sleeved red
Three-phase livesBrown, black, greyRed, yellow, blue

The trap: old black was neutral; new black is a phase conductor. Old blue was a phase; new blue is neutral. Where both schemes meet, the Regulations require a warning label at the board, and you must test rather than trust a colour.

On the extra-low-voltage side of an LED driver or bell transformer, red (+) and black (−) are conventional. Keep ELV wiring physically separated from mains terminals.

4. Cable sizes and breakers

Domestic wiring is mostly PVC-insulated single cores in conduit, or PVC twin-and-earth. The conductor size is chosen so the cable can carry the breaker's rating without overheating, with the installation method taken into account. Typical pairings:

CircuitConductorMCBNote
Lighting1.5 mm²6 A or 10 AType B. One circuit per floor is common.
Socket ring final2.5 mm²32 AAllowed on 32 A only because the ring shares current between two legs.
Socket radial2.5 mm²20 AUp to about 50 m² floor area.
Water heater, air-conditioner2.5 mm² or 4 mm²20 ADedicated circuit with a double-pole isolator at the appliance.
Cooker, oven6 mm²32 ACooker control unit with DP switch.
EV charger6 mm² or 10 mm²32 A or 40 AType A or B RCD, separate circuit.

Breaker letters describe how fast the magnetic trip reacts: B trips at 3 to 5 times rated current (lighting, sockets), C at 5 to 10 times (motors, air-conditioner compressors, workshop sockets). Fitting a C-type where a B-type belongs makes a fault take longer to clear.

5. The circuits in a Malaysian home

Lighting

A permanent live loops from fitting to fitting (or from switch to switch, in conduit installations). At each light, a switch drop takes the live down to the switch and the switched live back up to the lamp. Neutrals never visit a one-way switch; they are joined at the fitting or in a connector block. Try: 1-way switch, ceiling rose

Two-way lighting

Staircases and long corridors use two 2-way switches. Permanent live goes to COM of the first switch, two strappers join L1 to L1 and L2 to L2, and the switched live leaves from COM of the second switch. Either switch then changes the lamp state. Add intermediate switches between them for three or more positions. Try: 2-way staircase

Socket outlets

13 A switched sockets to BS 1363 / MS 589 on a ring final or a radial. On a ring, every socket terminal receives two conductors, one from each leg. Spurs off the ring are allowed, one unfused spur per socket. Try: 13 A socket, ring final

Fixed appliances

Water heaters, air-conditioners, cooker hoods and the like are fed through a fused connection unit (FCU) or a double-pole isolator, not a plug. The FCU adds a local fuse sized for the appliance; the DP isolator breaks both live and neutral so the appliance is truly dead for maintenance. Try: DP isolator, fused spur

LED and ELV lighting

LED strips and some downlights run on 12 V or 24 V DC from a driver. The driver's mains side is wired like any other load, switched in the live. Its output is SELV: safe to touch, but polarity-sensitive and unforgiving of mains contact. Try: switch + LED strip

Orientation matters too. Looking at the back of a BS 1363 socket, the earth is at the top, live on the left and neutral on the right, the mirror image of the front.

7. Switching rules

  • Switch the live, never the neutral. A switch in the neutral turns the lamp off but leaves the lampholder at 240 V. MS IEC 60364-5-53.
  • Fuses go in the live. Same reason. The plug fuse, the FCU fuse and the MCB all sit in the live conductor.
  • Double-pole for wet areas and fixed appliances. Water heaters, air-conditioners and cookers get a DP isolator that breaks L and N together.
  • Sleeve switched lives. A blue core used as a switch return is a live; mark it brown at both ends.
  • Neutrals do not visit a one-way switch. If you find a neutral in a switch box, it is either a loop-through to another fitting or a mistake.

8. Earthing and RCDs

Earthing has one purpose: when insulation fails and a live conductor touches metal, the earth gives that current a low-resistance path back to the supply. The current is then large enough to open the MCB in well under a second. Without earth, the metal sits at 240 V until someone touches it.

An RCD adds a second layer. It compares live and neutral current; any difference above 30 mA (current leaking to earth, possibly through a person) opens it within about 40 ms. Suruhanjaya Tenaga requires RCD protection on domestic installations. The RCD does not detect reversed polarity, overloads or L–N shorts; those are the MCB's job.

  • Earth every metal backbox, even behind a plastic plate.
  • Earth every class I appliance (anything with a metal case and an earth terminal). A class II (double-insulated, square-in-square symbol) appliance has no earth terminal and needs none.
  • Never join neutral to earth in a circuit. They meet once, at the intake.
  • Bond incoming metal water and gas pipes to the main earth terminal.

9. Safe isolation

Every job on the bench begins with the breaker already off. On a real job you make that true yourself, and you prove it:

  1. Identify the circuit and tell anyone affected.
  2. Switch off the MCB (or the main switch if unsure) and lock it off or tape it with a notice.
  3. Prove the tester on a known live source or a proving unit.
  4. Test dead at the point of work: L–N, L–E, N–E.
  5. Prove the tester again. A dead tester reads zero on a live circuit.

Do not use a neon screwdriver as a test instrument. Remember that a ceiling rose's LOOP terminal and a 2-way switch's strappers can be live from a different circuit than the one you switched off.

10. Testing before energising

Wiring correctly is half the job. Proving it is the other half, and the Electricity Regulations require installations to be tested before they are put into service. Dead tests first, live tests after.

A plug-in socket tester is a quick sanity check, not a test. It cannot see a broken ring, a high-impedance earth or a shared neutral.

12. Tools and good habits

  • VDE-insulated screwdrivers and side cutters; a two-pole voltage tester with a proving unit; a multifunction tester for the tests above.
  • Strip only as much insulation as the terminal depth. No bare copper outside the terminal, no insulation trapped under the screw.
  • Two conductors per terminal is normal; three is the usual maximum. Lay cores side by side, never twisted.
  • Tighten, then tug-test every core. Loose terminals are the leading cause of overheating at accessories.
  • Leave slack in the box. A cable cut to the millimetre cannot be re-terminated later.
  • Label anything unusual: mixed colour schemes, borrowed neutrals, circuits fed from another board.
  • Close up only after testing, and record the results.
